The Resident Services and Public Engagement Committee manages the City Council's communication strategies and public outreach initiatives to improve resident access to municipal government. Its work consistently centers on the production of a regular council newsletter and the facilitation of direct engagement through listening sessions and neighborhood meetings. Over the 2024-2025 and 2026-2027 terms, the committee has also reviewed major ordinance updates regarding human rights, immigration policy, and noise regulations. At its most recent meetings, the committee has focused on executing a city-wide tour of neighborhood ward meetings and auditing the city website for accessibility and accuracy.

ongoing

Human Rights Commission Modernization

settled

The committee reviewed and updated the enabling ordinance for the Human Rights Commission to modernize its operations. Key discussions included membership size, officer roles, and the establishment of member stipends. The final proposal, which set membership at 11 individuals with an annual stipend of $1,250, was referred to the full City Council in May 2025.

Noise Ordinance Update

ongoing

In late 2026, the committee began reviewing a proposal to update the city's 1974 noise ordinance, citing challenges with current enforcement. Councilor Tseng presented a draft based on a peer municipality's model that utilizes measurable decibel limits and a non-criminal fine structure. The matter remains in committee pending a public survey and consultation with the police department and local businesses.

Welcoming City Ordinance

settled

The committee held a public hearing on the Welcoming City Ordinance, which codifies non-cooperation with federal immigration authorities on non-criminal matters. After a period of public comment involving residents and advocacy groups, the committee recommended the ordinance for adoption with minor technical amendments.
