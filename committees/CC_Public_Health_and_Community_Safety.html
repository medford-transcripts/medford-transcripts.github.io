Public surveillance oversight and CCOPS reporting

ongoing

The committee amended the Community Control Over Public Surveillance (CCOPS) ordinance to strengthen privacy protections, regulate the destruction of illegally obtained data, and remove law enforcement exemptions for bulk data acquisition [M01#1]. It then reviewed the 2024 Annual Surveillance Report on body-worn cameras, examining internal audit findings that officers failed to notify civilians of recording in roughly one-third of audited instances [M04#1, M04#2]. After deferring action on parking and police surveillance data due to the absence of police representatives [M10#1], the committee reviewed the 2025 CCOPS annual report detailing body camera statistics and external surveillance data usage, ultimately approving the report while keeping the paper in committee [M11#1, M11#2].

Gender-affirming and reproductive healthcare protections

settled

The committee initiated review of a draft ordinance establishing Medford as a sanctuary for reproductive and gender-affirming healthcare to shield providers and recipients from out-of-state legal exposure [M02#1]. Following legal review, members amended the definition of 'City Agent' to include officials and contractors while removing grant recipients, and struck language requiring medical history or documentation from the definition of gender identity [M03#2, M03#3]. The committee then approved an updated purpose statement against using city resources for out-of-state healthcare enforcement and referred the amended ordinance to the full City Council for adoption [M03#5].

Immigration enforcement accountability and Welcoming City compliance

ongoing

The committee evaluated municipal interactions with federal law enforcement after learning federal agents used the police station parking lot for an independent operation, prompting inquiries into local notification procedures [M04#3]. Members subsequently approved a motion requesting the City Solicitor explore Freedom of Information Act requests to identify detained residents and discussed logistical support for impacted families [M05#2]. The committee later examined the Medford Police Department's report under the Welcoming City Ordinance, focusing on tracking federal immigration detainer requests while safeguarding resident privacy [M07#1].

Property overgrowth and rodent control regulations

settled

Councillor Collins introduced a new property overgrowth ordinance alongside revisions to rodent control rules to address public health hazards and fund mitigation through fines [M06#1]. The committee evaluated legal guidance to place specific rodent rules within Board of Health regulations and coordinated with the Department of Public Works to prevent overlapping enforcement on sidewalk obstructions [M06#2, M06#3]. The committee subsequently approved and reported out both measures, redirecting overgrowth penalty fines to the general operating fund and updating rodent control rules regarding composting, permit fees, and scaled property fines [M08#1, M08#2].

Law enforcement identification and masking regulations

settled

The committee introduced Paper 26-031, a resolution seeking to require law enforcement identification and protect Fourth Amendment rights [M09#1]. Members voted to retain the resolution in committee to refine legal language and solicit feedback from the police department, legal advocates, and community organizations [M09#1]. The committee then approved an ordinance prohibiting on-duty officers from wearing masks or disguises except under specific medical, tactical, religious, or weather exemptions, while establishing identification and identity-verification protocols during enforcement actions [M12#1].
