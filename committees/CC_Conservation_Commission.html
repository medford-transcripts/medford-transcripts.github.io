The Medford Conservation Commission is responsible for reviewing and permitting activities that impact wetland resource areas, floodplains, and riverfronts under state and local regulations. Throughout the record, the body has managed projects ranging from dam safety improvements and large-scale residential redevelopments to municipal park upgrades and trail restorations. Frequent points of discussion include invasive species management, stormwater calculations, tree preservation, and the use of native vegetation. The commission also performs ongoing compliance monitoring through site visits and the issuance of Certificates of Compliance. Most recently, the body has focused on drafting comprehensive new local wetlands regulations to establish more stringent environmental protections.

Local Wetlands Protection Regulations

ongoing

The commission initiated a detailed section-by-section review of a 42-page draft of local wetlands protection regulations intended to supplement the state's Wetlands Protection Act. The draft includes proposals for stricter two-to-one wetland replication ratios, a 25-foot no-activity zone, and prohibitions on certain hazardous construction materials like coal tar-based sealants. Members have debated the practical impacts of withholding Certificates of Compliance to monitor plant success and how to best incentivize tree preservation through financial penalties or replacement formulas. As of late 2026, the commission continues to refine these regulations in dedicated work sessions.

South Reservoir Dam Improvements

settled

The commission reviewed a Notice of Intent from the Town of Winchester for safety repairs and dam rehabilitation at the South Reservoir. Early discussions addressed the project's impact on bordering vegetated wetlands and the necessity of state and federal agency reviews. The project was eventually approved with an Order of Conditions that required a one-to-three tree replication ratio and a pre-construction invasive species management plan. This work also established a formal operations and maintenance plan for the wetland buffer area.

Large-Scale Residential Development

settled

Significant reviews were conducted for multi-family developments at 28 Sycamore Avenue and 97-99 Revere Beach Parkway. The Sycamore Avenue project required revisions to reduce parking and increase compensatory flood storage to mitigate abutter concerns about high groundwater. The Revere Beach Parkway redevelopment, located at a former radio station site, was approved with conditions for porous pavement and the preservation of specific native tree groves along the Malden River. Both projects emphasized public access to greenways and robust herbicide protocols for managing invasive species.
