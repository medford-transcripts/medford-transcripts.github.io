The Medford Conservation Commission is responsible for protecting the city's wetland resource areas through the administration of state and local environmental regulations. It reviews notices of intent for redevelopment, issues determinations for municipal and private projects, and monitors compliance with conservation conditions through site inspections. Over the span of 2022 to 2026, the body has transitioned from overseeing specific residential and park projects to drafting comprehensive local wetlands protection regulations. As of October 2026, the commission is finalizing this new regulatory framework while continuing to oversee habitat restoration and infrastructure projects in the Middlesex Fells Reservation.

Local Wetlands Protection Regulations

ongoing

The commission initiated a major effort to modernize local oversight by drafting a 42-page set of regulations based on high-performance standards from neighboring municipalities (M24#1). These draft rules propose stricter protections than the state's Wetlands Protection Act, including a potential two-to-one wetland replication ratio and a 25-foot no-activity zone (M24#5, M24#9). Discussion has also focused on prohibiting hazardous materials like coal tar-based sealants and establishing tree replacement formulas for developers (M24#4, M24#8). The commission continues to hold dedicated sessions to finalize these regulations (M27#5).

ongoing

The body also issues certificates of compliance to close out long-standing projects, such as a 2014 stream crossing (M27#3).

Stormwater and Flood Mitigation

ongoing

The commission regularly evaluates how new developments impact local drainage and flood storage capacity.
