The CC Planning and Permitting committee manages the modernization and overhaul of the city's zoning ordinances and land use regulations. Its primary responsibilities include refining zoning definitions, creating new corridor-specific districts, and establishing development standards for residential and commercial growth. The body began its work in May 2024 by prioritizing technical updates and has since moved through neighborhood-by-neighborhood rezoning efforts. As of May 2026, the committee has most recently referred a major rezoning proposal for the Boston Avenue corridor and Tufts University area for joint review with the Community Development Board.

Later sessions addressed technical cleanup of procedural language and the alignment of local codes with state mandates. Ongoing work includes mapping analysis and periodic technical updates to ensure citywide consistency across new sub-districts.

Mystic Avenue Corridor Rezoning

settled

Beginning in mid-2024, the committee focused on transforming the auto-oriented Mystic Avenue into a walkable mixed-use corridor. Proposals introduced tiered building heights ranging from six to twelve stories and 'curb setbacks' to facilitate wider sidewalks on private property. The committee approved a specific model using incentive zoning to trade increased height for community benefits. The final proposal, which established four new sub-districts, was referred to the full City Council in October 2024.

Special attention was paid to ensuring that existing non-conforming homes could be renovated without requiring variances. Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs)

settled

To comply with new state mandates, the committee developed a local ordinance governing Accessory Dwelling Units. Members debated discretionary regulations such as setback requirements, height limits for detached units, and a ban on short-term rentals. The body also established special permit requirements for properties seeking to add a second ADU or utilize historic accessory structures. The final set of ADU regulations was referred favorably in April 2025.

settled

Medford and West Medford Squares

settled

Zoning for the city's commercial squares was updated to increase residential density near transit hubs. For Medford Square, the committee established 'daylight standards' and angular planes to protect sunlight for abutting homes, while West Medford Square was rezoned to incentivize housing near the commuter rail. Both square proposals were referred to the Community Development Board in April 2025. By 2026, the city also began exploring 40R 'Smart Growth' overlays for Medford Square to unlock state funding for the high school project.

ongoing
