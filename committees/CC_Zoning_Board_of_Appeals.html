The CC Zoning Board of Appeals is a quasi-judicial body responsible for adjudicating requests for relief from the Medford Zoning Ordinance through variances and special permits. It reviews residential expansions, commercial modifications, and large-scale affordable housing projects under MGL Chapter 40B, which allows for the bypass of certain local zoning regulations. Between July 2023 and September 2026, the board managed a high volume of petitions ranging from minor dimensional setbacks to multi-hundred-unit developments. As of the most recent meetings, the board is navigating the implementation of new state regulations regarding Accessory Dwelling Units and overseeing the final design refinements of major housing projects.

Residential Density and Multi-Family Conversions

ongoing

Property owners frequently petition to increase housing density by converting single-family homes into multi-unit dwellings. The board evaluates these based on lot area, parking capacity, and statutory hardship, leading to approvals for historic carriage house additions and the conversion of ground-floor commercial space into apartments. However, petitions are denied when the board finds a lack of compelling hardship or potential detriment to the neighborhood character. Recent cases show a trend toward higher-density townhomes replacing former commercial automotive sites.

Dimensional Relief for Fencing and Privacy

settled

The board adjudicates numerous requests for over-height fences, often triggered by topographical differences between properties or security needs for commercial utilities. For residential lots, the board has granted variances for eight-foot fences to ensure privacy when standard six-foot limits are insufficient due to site elevation. Commercial sites, such as the Medford Housing Authority and industrial facilities, have received permits for high-security fencing and generator enclosures. The board occasionally denies these requests if the structures are deemed unsightly or impede traffic visibility at intersections.

Flood Mitigation and Topographical Hardships

ongoing

The board frequently grants relief for residential modifications intended to address site-specific physical issues like chronic flooding and irregular lot shapes. This includes allowing the infilling of below-grade garages and the creation of front-yard parking spaces where traditional driveways are unusable. Approvals in these cases often require the use of permeable pavers to manage drainage and consultation with the city's engineering department. The board cites the unique 'pie-shape' or 'diagonal orientation' of certain lots as the legal basis for granting these variances.
