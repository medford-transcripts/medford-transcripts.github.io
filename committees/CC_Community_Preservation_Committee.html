The Community Preservation Committee (CPC) is responsible for recommending the allocation of Community Preservation Act (CPA) funds toward open space, recreation, historic preservation, and affordable housing projects in Medford. Since its inaugural meetings in 2018, the committee has managed millions of dollars in local surcharges and state matching funds, developing rigorous application guidelines and project evaluation criteria. The body serves as a central hub for reviewing proposals from city departments, non-profits, and private historic property owners. As of October 2026, the committee is reviewing a new round of applications for the 2027 fiscal year while refining its strategies for long-term bonding and large-scale municipal projects.

Affordable Housing Initiatives

ongoing

Early work focused on individual unit construction and heating conversions for housing authority residents. The committee eventually transitioned to multi-year, multi-million dollar commitments for large redevelopments, most notably recommending $2.4 million for the Walkling Court project. Recent work includes the establishment of a dedicated Affordable Housing Trust Fund to manage seed money and homebuyer assistance programs. The committee also supports homelessness prevention through rental assistance grants.

Park and Playground Development

ongoing

The committee has consistently funded the design and construction of inclusive recreational spaces, including the $2.8 million universally accessible playground at McGlynn School. Major recurring projects include the phased renovation of Carr Park for pickleball and other amenities, and the reconstruction of tennis courts at Playstead Park. Funding has also been used to leverage larger state and federal grants through initial feasibility and design studies. Recently, the committee has expanded its focus to include athletic court resurfacing at Victory Park and athletic field lighting at Tufts Park.

Brooks Estate Restoration

ongoing

Restoration of the historic Shepherd Brooks Manor and its surrounding estate has been a core thread since the committee's inception. Funded projects range from invasive species management and trail restoration to major structural work on the manor's east entryways and wood trim. The committee has also supported professional engineering for the estate's historic 1,900-foot access drive. Most recently, the committee approved a small grant for an archaeological sensitivity report covering the estate and a neighboring cemetery parcel.

Historic Cemetery Preservation

ongoing

The committee oversees ongoing preservation efforts at Oak Grove, Cross Street, and the Salem Street Burial Ground. Early projects involved landscape rehabilitation and wall repairs, evolving into a comprehensive master planning study for cemetery buildings and caretakers' quarters. Significant funding has been recommended for the restoration of the Angel of Victory and Peace fountain and veteran headstones. Ongoing discussions involve the long-term strategic plan for cemetery structures, weighing restoration against the construction of new operational facilities.

Municipal Building and Infrastructure Maintenance

ongoing

Municipal preservation has increasingly focused on City Hall, including restroom ADA compliance and historically accurate window restorations in the City Council chambers. The committee has also funded repairs for the Condon Shell and studied potential uses for the Curtis Tufts school and the Hegner Center. In 2026, the committee began reviewing a large request for City Hall limestone facade repairs. Private Property and Religious Institution Preservation

ongoing

The committee provides grants for historic preservation on non-municipal properties, including several local churches and sites like the Royall House and Isaac Hall House. Projects often include exterior siding, wood trim restoration, and stained-glass window repairs at Shiloh Baptist and Unitarian Universalist churches. These grants are typically conditioned on preservation restrictions and the exclusion of religious imagery from funded elements. Recent deliberations have focused on establishing stricter guidelines for public funding of private property to ensure functional community use.

Administrative Policy and Grant Management

ongoing

The committee continuously refines its administrative operations, including the hiring of a full-time CPA manager in 2023. Key policy changes include increasing the small grant limit from $5,000 to $10,000 and removing mandatory matching requirements to increase accessibility. The committee also establishes annual budget set-asides, recently increasing the minimum allocation for affordable housing to 15%. Ongoing efforts include improving application transparency through online templates and public outreach via QR codes and community events.
