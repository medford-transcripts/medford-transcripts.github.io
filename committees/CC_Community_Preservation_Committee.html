The Medford Community Preservation Committee recommends the allocation of Community Preservation Act funds for community housing, historic preservation, and open space and recreation projects. The archive records 27 meetings from April 2022 through October 2026. Over this span, the committee has established annual budget set-asides, managed standard and off-cycle grant applications, and monitored previously funded civic projects. Funding decisions regularly incorporate conditions such as historic preservation restrictions, maintenance agreements, and public access requirements. At its most recent meeting in October 2026, the committee began reviewing fiscal year 2027 funding applications spanning rental assistance, City Hall facade restorations, and athletic court resurfacing.

Walkling Court affordable housing redevelopment

ongoing

City officials identified the Walkling Court redevelopment as an affordable housing priority in early 2022 before the Medford Housing Authority submitted a multi-million-dollar funding request [M01#3, M05#7]. The committee recommended $800,000 for Phase 1 pre-development costs as a forgivable loan conditioned on securing zoning approvals for 54 affordable units [M08#1]. Later that year, the committee approved an additional $800,000 request to complete pre-development and construction financing [M11#3]. By mid-2026, housing authority representatives reported that a guaranteed maximum price contract had been finalized and Phase 1 construction was slated to begin in late summer 2026 [M24#4].

McGlynn School inclusive playground

settled

Following testimony on school playground accessibility, proponents requested $900,000 in CPA funds toward a universally accessible playground at the McGlynn School [M01#3, M05#4]. The committee initially recommended $500,000 with conditions guaranteeing public access outside school hours and requiring a preventative maintenance plan [M07#4]. An additional $400,000 was recommended and approved for Phase 2 construction, with conditions prohibiting the use of CPA funds for artificial turf [M09#6, M11#4]. In 2024, the committee approved a $5,000 small grant for an accessible gaga ball pit at the school and received a project update confirming that the playground was nearing completion [M14#3, M14#4].

Shiloh Baptist Church restoration and accessibility

ongoing

Shiloh Baptist Church initially applied for $150,000 in 2022 to restore historic stained-glass windows, receiving a funding recommendation conditioned on a preservation restriction and omitting religious imagery from funded panels [M05#6, M07#2]. In 2024, an off-cycle application for church accessibility improvements and daycare expansion was declined because it did not meet emergency funding criteria, returning the request to the standard funding cycle [M16#1]. The committee subsequently recommended $400,000 for ADA improvements and building preservation [M19#9]. The church later requested and was awarded $150,000 for Phase 2 stained glass and window restoration, subject to the submission of schematic drawings and carpentry estimates [M22#2, M26#5].

Historic cemetery restoration and monuments

ongoing

The committee began cemetery preservation efforts by funding feasibility and master planning studies for the Oak Grove Cemetery caretakers' quarters [M04#2, M07#3]. The committee later recommended $85,000 for Oak Grove building restoration planning and approved $5,000 for restoring veteran headstones at Cross Street Cemetery [M19#8, M20#1]. Proposals subsequently advanced for restoring the historic Angel of Victory and Peace Fountain and undertaking broader cemetery building preservations [M22#1, M22#6]. In 2026, the committee approved $167,800 to restore the fountain and $126,500 for monument and wall repairs in Phase 2 of the Cross Street Cemetery restoration [M26#3, M26#4].

City Hall and municipal facilities preservation

ongoing

The committee subsequently approved $322,500 for historic window restoration in the City Council chambers, conditioned on local historic district designation efforts, and recommended $37,280 for emergency repairs at the Medford Armory [M19#7, M21#1]. By late 2026, new applications sought $300,000 for City Hall facade restoration and $51,000 for accessible drinking fountains [M27#2, M27#3].
