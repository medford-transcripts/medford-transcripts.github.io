The Medford Commission for Persons with Disabilities is a municipal advisory body tasked with promoting equal status, accessibility, and public resources for disabled residents across Medford. Operating continuously from July 2022 through September 2026 across three terms, the commission advises city officials and municipal departments on physical and digital compliance under the Americans with Disabilities Act. Its ongoing portfolio includes coordinating annual disability resource and pride fairs, reviewing accessibility in public parks and municipal facilities, advocating for transit and pedestrian infrastructure upgrades, and monitoring the city's comprehensive ADA transition plan. At its most recent meetings, the commission prioritized intersections for casino mitigation grant-funded accessible pedestrian signals and reviewed municipal facility and sidewalk surveys conducted by the Institute for Human Centered Design.

Sidewalks, curb cuts, and pedestrian signals

ongoing

The commission began tracking municipal pedestrian infrastructure in 2022, identifying non-navigable sidewalk paving along Fellsway West and a lack of curb cuts near key commercial intersections [M03#5]. The commission subsequently approved support for a municipal grant application prioritizing curb cuts near Medford High School and public spaces [M15#3]. Over subsequent sessions, members reviewed code enforcement trends regarding sidewalk snow obstructions and studied sidewalk tripping hazards caused by tree planting [M18#9, M22#7]. By 2026, the body partnered with Director of Traffic and Transportation Todd Blake to install accessible pedestrian signal buttons and prioritized candidate intersections for upgrades funded by a casino mitigation grant [M24#4, M25#3].

Municipal buildings and facility upgrades

ongoing

Early facility work targeted ADA deficiencies at Medford High School, including non-compliant entrances, speed bump obstacles, and obstructed curb cuts [M03#6, M05#4]. The commission voted to support appointing a commissioner to the Medford High School Building Committee to ensure ongoing accessibility oversight [M15#2]. In City Hall, the commission used ARPA funding to acquire accessible parking stencils, assistive listening receivers, and cushioned armchairs for public chambers [M04#4, M18#3, M19#3]. The commission also reviewed elevator repairs and tracked progress on the design phase for fully accessible public restrooms in City Hall [M18#7, M19#3, M20#5].

ongoing
