The Medford Traffic Commission is a public body responsible for managing citywide traffic safety, parking regulations, permit programs, and intersection modifications. Across 18 meetings from May 2023 through September 2026, the commission evaluated resident parking variance requests, implemented crosswalk daylighting restrictions, established designated handicap parking spaces, and revised municipal parking fee structures. Major decisions included establishing and permanently adopting the G-Zone transit parking program and prohibiting emergency left turns on Emerson Street following pilot safety reviews. The body also instituted landlord permits, adjusted visitor pass fees, and created safety restrictions for emergency vehicle navigation. At its September 2026 meeting, the commission approved multiple residential parking variances, established new resident permit parking on Quincy Street, and implemented corner parking restrictions to accommodate Fire Department access and sightlines.

Resident parking variances

The commission regularly reviews petitions from residents seeking parking variances on adjacent streets due to lack of off-street parking or restricted primary addresses. Early decisions included denying a vacation storage request on Mystic Street while approving variances on Ravine Road and Ross Street. In October 2024, the body established a policy making resident parking variances permanent for current holders rather than requiring annual renewals. The commission continued to evaluate individual petitions through late 2026, granting variances for locations on Oakland Street, North Street, and Bow Street while denying or tabling others where nearby parking options were available.

Transit area parking and G-Zone implementation

settled

The commission initially managed transit accessibility by positioning accessible parking spaces and ride-share zones near the Boston Avenue Green Line Extension station. In April 2024, the commission approved a one-year pilot program establishing a unified resident parking 'G-Zone' in South Medford near transit stations, incorporating quarterly public reviews. Following a review period showing zero formal complaints and effective enforcement, the commission voted in February 2025 to make the G-Zone residential parking program permanent.

Initial measures restricted parking near crosswalks on Medford Street, Salem Street, and High Street. Commercial vehicle overnight parking regulations

ongoing

The commission addresses requests and restrictions regarding overnight commercial vehicle parking on municipal streets. After granting individual commercial pickup variances and creating no-overnight storage tow zones on Commercial Street and Mystic Avenue, the commission strictly enforced a 2002 city ordinance prohibiting overnight commercial vehicle parking. Requests for commercial vehicle variances on Holton Street, Burgett Avenue, and Sharon Street were tabled or denied under this rule. The commission later opened discussions in July 2025 regarding potential permit updates for certain commercial vehicles meeting specific size criteria.

Intersection controls, lane structures, and traffic restrictions

ongoing

The commission approves street conversions, turn restrictions, and stop signs to manage vehicle speeds and accident hotspots. In late 2023, the body approved a three-way stop at Bowen and Dexter Streets and an emergency left-turn restriction from Emerson Street onto Main Street. Following a trial period that successfully reduced collisions, the Emerson Street left-turn restriction was made permanent in February 2025. The commission also implemented rush-hour parking limits and lane formalization on Forest Street, converted Warren Street and Cooley Road to a four-way stop, and approved a stop sign at Stearns and College Avenues.
