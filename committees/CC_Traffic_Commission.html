The CC Traffic Commission is a public body responsible for managing parking regulations, traffic safety, street designations, permit policies, and municipal lot rules in Medford. Meeting from December 2022 through September 2026, the commission adjudicates residential parking variance requests, establishes speed limits and stop signs, implements crosswalk daylighting, and regulates commercial vehicle parking. Key milestones include approving permanent residential permit parking programs in response to the Green Line Extension, standardizing fee structures, and designating electric vehicle charging locations. As of its September 2026 meeting, the commission continues to process neighborhood parking petitions, enact emergency vehicle access restrictions, and update pass replacement fee schedules.

Green Line Extension and G-Zone permit parking

settled

In response to the Green Line Extension, the commission initially approved neighborhood opt-out petitions exempting specific streets from proposed permit parking restrictions [M01#10, M04#2] and rejected a broader half-mile expansion proposal [M02#11]. In April 2024, the commission established a one-year pilot program creating a unified resident parking zone in South Medford near transit stations [M14#2]. Following a trial period with quarterly reviews and no formal complaints, the commission voted in February 2025 to make the G-Zone residential parking program permanent [M19#15].

Winthrop Street traffic and bike lane design

settled

The commission addressed traffic safety along Winthrop Street by establishing a 20 mph school zone near Medford High School [M02#10]. A proposal for Phase II bike lanes was initially tabled over concerns regarding residential parking and rotary slip lanes [M04#4]. In April 2023, the commission approved an alternative bike lane design extending from Lawrence Road to South Street that preserved on-street parking and restored specific right-turn lanes at Winthrop Circle [M05#3]. The commission later rejected resident petitions to remove no-parking signs and restore street parking along the corridor, upholding the installed bike lanes [M08#1].

Intersection sightlines and crosswalk daylighting

ongoing

The commission routinely addresses sightline safety by approving crosswalk daylighting restrictions that eliminate parking spaces adjacent to intersections [M03#4, M08#2, M09#5]. It implemented similar safety zones across various neighborhoods to improve pedestrian visibility, including along Lawrence Road and High Street [M16#7, M19#13, M22#10]. The body also directed daylighting near commuter transit crosswalks on Playstead Road [M21#9]. In September 2026, the commission enacted corner parking restrictions on Walnut, Thomas, and Hancock Streets to maintain driver sightlines [M23#13, M23#14].

Residential parking permits and variances

ongoing

Throughout its term, the commission considers individual petitions from residents seeking parking permit variances or visitor passes on adjacent streets due to lack of off-street parking or primary street restrictions [M01#4, M01#7, M13#2]. To streamline reviews, the commission adopted a policy in October 2024 making residential parking variances permanent for current residents as long as permits remain continuously active [M15#10]. It also authorized street-wide permit parking regulations, such as on Pinkham Road and Quincy Street [M22#7, M23#5]. The commission continues to approve and deny individual variance requests as issues arise [M23#2, M23#3, M23#6].

Commercial vehicle parking regulations

ongoing

The commission evaluates restrictions on commercial vehicle parking across the city, initially granting occasional individual overnight variances for unlettered or work vehicles [M09#7, M11#4]. To combat vehicle storage, the body instituted overnight tow zones on Commercial Street and Mystic Avenue [M11#7, M12#9]. In early 2025, the commission denied multiple commercial variance requests, citing a city ordinance prohibiting overnight commercial parking on public streets [M19#8, M19#10]. It subsequently discussed proposed revisions to allow residential permits for certain commercial vehicles meeting size criteria [M20#5].

On-street electric vehicle charging stations

settled

In September 2025, the commission reviewed a grant-funded proposal to install on-street electric vehicle charging stations across five city parks and received feedback from the public regarding neighborhood traffic and parking impacts [M21#10]. The following month, the commission approved two on-street charging stations near 200 Boston Avenue as a compromise location serving neighborhoods with limited off-street parking [M22#2].

Parking fee schedules and municipal lot management

ongoing

The commission manages municipal parking lots and fee schedules, designating spaces in the Upper City Hall lot for commuter and paid parking [M02#8] and designating the Clippership East lot for business permit parking [M16#10]. In November 2024, the commission approved a revised permit fee structure establishing tiered residential permit pricing and increases for business and commuter permits [M16#12]. It later restricted 71 spaces in the south City Hall lot exclusively to municipal employees during business hours [M18#10]. In September 2026, the commission voted to increase replacement fees for lost visitor passes starting in the 2027 permit year [M23#12].
