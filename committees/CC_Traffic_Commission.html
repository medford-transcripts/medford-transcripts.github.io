The Medford Traffic Commission is a municipal regulatory body responsible for establishing and managing traffic rules, parking regulations, street signage, and roadway safety infrastructure across the City of Medford. The commission's work includes reviewing residential parking permit zones, granting individual parking variances, implementing traffic calming and bicycle facilities, approving accessible parking spaces, and enacting intersection controls. The archive holds 28 meetings spanning from August 16, 2022, through September 8, 2026. Over this span, the commission developed and finalized a permanent residential parking program around Green Line Extension stations, restructured parking fee schedules, and systematically implemented intersection daylighting and emergency access restrictions. At its most recent meeting in September 2026, the commission approved block-level permit parking on Quincy Street, raised replacement fees for visitor passes, and enacted parking tow zones to ensure fire department apparatus access.

Lines of work

Green Line transit area resident parking and the G-Zone

settled

The commission initially established a resident permit parking pilot for unrestricted public ways within a quarter-mile of new Green Line Extension stations [M01#1]. Proposals to proactively expand the pilot to a half-mile radius were repeatedly tabled and ultimately denied following neighborhood opt-out petitions and resident opposition [M02#2, M05#10, M06#11, M08#2]. In 2024, the commission approved a one-year pilot establishing a unified transit area parking zone in South Medford known as the GL Zone [M18#2]. Following a trial period with no formal complaints and active enforcement, the commission voted to make the G-Zone residential parking program permanent [M23#15], subsequently permitting residents facing metered frontages to park within the zone [M27#3].

Winthrop Street traffic calming and bicycle lanes

settled

The commission approved Phase I of buffered bike lanes and speed tables along Winthrop Street from the Winchester line to Lawrence Road [M01#2]. When Phase II came before the commission, members tabled the proposal to address neighborhood concerns over the removal of rotary slip lanes and the loss of residential parking [M08#4]. The body subsequently approved an alternative design for Phase II that retained resident parking and right-turn lanes at Winthrop Circle while implementing buffered bike lanes near the community garden [M09#3]. Subsequent resident petitions seeking to remove no-parking signs and restore street parking along the corridor were placed on file to uphold complete streets policies and bike lane regulations [M12#1].

Crosswalk daylighting and intersection sightline restrictions

ongoing

The commission systematically implemented daylighting restrictions to eliminate parking spaces approaching crosswalks and blind intersections, beginning with parking removals on Fulton Street and High Street [M07#4]. Additional parking setbacks and pavement hatching were authorized at intersections including Medford Street at Broadway, Salem and Fulton Streets, and along High Street [M12#2, M12#3, M13#5]. The body continued applying clearance buffers at locations such as Fulton Street at Maple Park Avenue, Lawrence Road at Rural Avenue, and High Street at Powderhouse Road [M18#6, M20#7, M23#13]. Later actions enacted sightline parking restrictions at Playstead Road, Washington Street, and multiple corners around the Hancock Street area [M25#9, M27#4, M28#14].

Commercial vehicle parking regulations

ongoing

The commission handled varying requests for commercial vehicle overnight parking exemptions, occasionally granting individual variances for unlettered vehicles while enacting overnight truck tow zones on corridors like Commercial Street and Mystic Avenue [M13#7, M15#4, M15#7, M16#9]. A request to permit overnight parking for a commercial-plated passenger sedan was later tabled pending broader administrative and ordinance solutions [M22#8]. Citing a 2002 city ordinance prohibiting overnight commercial street parking, the commission subsequently denied multiple commercial vehicle variance applications [M23#8, M23#10]. The body then initiated discussions on updating regulations to allow residential permits for smaller commercial vehicles meeting specific size criteria, referring the proposal for public outreach [M24#5].

Parking permit policies, fees, and variance administration

ongoing

The commission adopted rules establishing permit access for absentee landlords and recommended free visitor passes for non-vehicle seniors to the City Council [M06#3, M06#7]. To reduce administrative overhead, the body enacted a policy making residential parking variances permanent for current residents provided permits are consistently exercised [M19#10]. In late 2024, the commission created an annual landlord parking permit program and approved an updated fee structure establishing tiered pricing for residential permits alongside rate increases for business and commuter permits [M20#9, M20#12]. In 2026, the commission approved increasing replacement fees for lost visitor passes to take effect in the 2027 permit year [M28#12].

Municipal parking lots and paid parking management

ongoing

The commission established parking regulations across municipal off-street facilities, establishing daytime limits and business permits in the Tufts Park lot and designating 23 commuter parking spaces in the Upper City Hall lot [M05#3, M06#8]. Further actions included relocating accessible spaces in the Yale Street lot and designating the Clippership East lot for business permit use [M14#4, M20#10]. In 2025, the body converted 71 spaces in the south City Hall lot to permit-only parking for municipal employees during business hours and approved a waste storage agreement in the Clippership lot [M22#10, M23#9].

Stop sign installations and turning restrictions

ongoing

The commission addressed hazardous intersections by establishing turn restrictions and stop signs, beginning with No Turn on Red restrictions at Forest Street and Lawrence Road [M01#5]. After converting Bowen and Dexter Streets into a three-way stop, the commission enacted an emergency left-turn restriction from Emerson Street onto Main Street to curb collisions [M13#3, M14#3]. Following a trial period showing a measurable reduction in crashes, the body voted to make the Emerson Street left-turn prohibition permanent [M23#14]. The commission also approved new stop signs at multiple intersections, including Whittemore Street, Warren Street at Cooley Road, and Stearns Avenue at College Avenue [M20#13, M21#5, M28#9].
