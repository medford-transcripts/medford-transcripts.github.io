The Medford Traffic Commission regulates parking regulations, permit programs, traffic flow, and roadway safety across the city of Medford. The commission's record in the archive covers 13 meetings held between December 2023 and September 2026. Throughout this period, the body has decided on residential permit variances, established transit parking zones, updated fee schedules, and enacted safety measures such as crosswalk daylighting. Residential parking permit variances

ongoing

The commission routinely evaluates variance requests from residents lacking off-street parking or living on unpermitted main streets who seek permits for adjacent side streets (M01#8, M02#3, M04#4). In October 2024, the body streamlined its workflow by adopting a policy that makes residential parking variances permanent for current residents as long as permits are maintained (M05#10). Despite this administrative policy, the commission continues to consider individual petitions and grant location-specific variances (M11#7, M12#3, M13#2).

Commercial vehicle parking restrictions

ongoing

To prevent commercial trucks and local businesses from using public streets for long-term vehicle storage, the commission instituted overnight parking bans on major corridors like Commercial Street and Mystic Avenue (M01#7, M02#9). The body occasionally granted temporary variances for commercial work vehicles (M01#4), but later denied multiple commercial variance requests by citing a city ordinance prohibiting overnight commercial street parking (M09#8). In July 2025, the commission initiated discussions on updating regulations to allow residential permits for certain smaller commercial vehicles, referring the proposal for public outreach (M10#5).

Crosswalk daylighting and intersection sightlines

ongoing

The commission frequently approves parking restrictions near intersections and crosswalks to improve driver visibility and pedestrian safety. Emergency vehicle clearance and access

ongoing

The commission acts on requests from the Fire Department and public safety officials to clear narrow streets and tight turns for emergency apparatus. In late 2023, the body created a tow zone on Foss Street to ensure emergency vehicles could pass safely (M01#3).
