The Medford Community Development Board is responsible for site plan reviews, special permit recommendations, and the evaluation of comprehensive zoning amendments across the city. Its work from 2020 to 2026 focused on a transformative overhaul of the city's zoning code to align with a newly adopted 2023 Comprehensive Plan. The board evaluates major commercial projects, specifically life science laboratories, mixed-use developments, and large-scale affordable housing initiatives. In its most recent meetings, the board has been finalizing specific corridor zoning districts and performing technical 'cleanup' on the zoning ordinance to standardize definitions and development incentives.

Comprehensive Zoning Overhaul

ongoing

The board began a systematic recodification of the 1965 zoning ordinance to modernize legal definitions and use tables. This effort expanded into district-specific rezoning for major corridors, including Mystic Avenue, Salem Street, and the Boston Avenue Neighborhood Corridor District. These amendments introduced 'Green Score' performance standards, daylighting protections, and incentive-based density bonuses. By late 2026, the board was focused on a final 'zoning cleanup' to standardize formatting and height definitions citywide.

Tufts University and Institutional Zoning

ongoing

Institutional projects were initially reviewed under the Dover Amendment, including new dormitories on Winthrop Street and tennis courts on College Avenue. As the scale of projects increased, notably a 10-story dormitory on Boston Avenue and a major aquatic center, the board moved to create a dedicated Tufts Institutional Zone. This new district aims to replace current zoning gaps with specific height limits and setbacks to protect abutting residential neighborhoods. The board also established standards for purpose-built student housing, including on-site management and occupancy requirements.

Affordable Housing and Walkling Court

settled

The board prioritized the redevelopment of Walkling Court by the Medford Housing Authority into a 238-unit deeply affordable residential complex. This required the creation of a specific Planned Development District (PDD-3) to allow for increased density across multiple buildings. Other housing work included advising on a 350-unit Chapter 40B development at 4000 Mystic Valley Parkway and modernization of the Saltonstall building. The board also reviewed and approved specialized shelter projects, such as Halo House on St. Clair Road.

Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs)

settled

The board approved Medford's first accessory dwelling unit in early 2021 and continued to process special permits for detached units above garages. Following new state legislation, the board held hearings to align local regulations with state mandates that protect ADUs in single-family zones. Deliberations focused on size restrictions, parking exemptions in transit-adjacent areas, and the feasibility of units in different residential typologies. The final framework allows for local regulation while ensuring compliance with state-wide protected status.

Linkage Fees and Inclusionary Housing

ongoing

The board manages the city's linkage fee schedule, which offsets the impacts of large-scale developments through payments for community benefits. The board initiated a formal evaluation of these fees to include potential stormwater impact assessments and annual inflation adjustments. In 2026, the board recommended an amendment to the inclusionary housing ordinance to allow for fractional cash-in-lieu payments to the Affordable Housing Trust. Work continues on refining the multiplier-based bonus system for developers providing deeper affordability or community amenities.
