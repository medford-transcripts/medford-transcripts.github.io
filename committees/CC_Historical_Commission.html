The CC Historical Commission and the Historic District Commission manage Medford's architectural and archaeological heritage through demolition delay enforcement and the oversight of local historic districts. Spanning a period from December 2022 to October 2026, the commission evaluates structures over 75 years old for historical significance and advises on public infrastructure projects through Section 106 reviews. The body's work encompasses significant archaeological site management, including colonial burial grounds and the Thomas Brooks Park master plan. By the most recent meeting, the commission was focused on integrating historic preservation incentives into city zoning and launching new neighborhood-wide historical inventory surveys.

South Street Historic District

settled

The commission initiated the process to create the South Street Shipbuilding Historic District by refining boundaries and hiring consultant Chris Skelly to draft the preliminary study report. Outreach involved community events like PorchFest and direct mailers to approximately 18 property owners to explain the benefits of designation. After revisions to architectural guidelines to allow for modern materials, the district was approved and its map filed with the Registrar of Deeds. Ongoing work includes the design and placement of permanent historical signage in collaboration with local students.

Archaeological Preservation at Thomas Brooks Park

ongoing

The commission has managed the long-term restoration of Thomas Brooks Park, starting with repairs to 'Pomp's Wall' to honor its enslaved builder and the fieldstone wall along Grove Street. Peter Hedlund presented multi-phase master plan concepts that include accessible history walks, interpretive signage, and an archaeological cap to protect the site of the former Brooks estate house. Phase two work, supported by Community Preservation Act funding, involves tree maintenance, ADA-compliant sidewalk connections, and further archaeological research. Public input sessions have focused on balancing neighborhood access with the preservation of the site's colonial and indigenous history.

Cemetery Assessment and Restoration

ongoing

Significant efforts have been directed toward the restoration of the Cross Street Burial Ground and Oak Grove Cemetery. Archaeological trenching at the former Cross Street parcel uncovered artifacts and human remains, necessitating multi-year coordination with state authorities. The commission approved funding for headstone foundation assessments and a pilot program for the restoration of veteran headstones. Plans include repairing masonry, cleaning walls, and recreating historical front gates using grant funding.

Demolition Review and Ordinance Reform

ongoing

The commission frequently adjudicates 'preferably preserved' status for residential and commercial properties, imposing 18-month demolition delays to encourage preservation or sympathetic redesign. Members have met with multiple Building Commissioners to discuss streamlining the review process and better defining 'partial demolition' within the city ordinance. Discussions have included proposals to grant automatic significance to buildings constructed before 1900 to expedite permitting for newer structures. Recent efforts focus on providing zoning relief for property owners who preserve historic structures while increasing density.

Historical Neighborhood Surveys

ongoing

To document the city's resources, the commission oversees neighborhood-wide inventory surveys that produce Massachusetts Historical Commission Form B reports. Surveys have been completed for the Brickyards, Fulton Heights, and Sagamore Vale areas, covering hundreds of individual buildings. The commission utilizes city budget matching funds and state grants to prioritize neighborhoods like Lawrence Estates and Hastings Heights for future documentation. This data is being integrated into a searchable local history database for public access.

Section 106 and Institutional Project Reviews

ongoing

The commission conducts reviews for major developments and infrastructure projects under Section 106, including the redevelopment of Walkling Court and repairs to Interstate 93 bridges. Members provided design feedback on the new Fire Department headquarters and the Tufts Aquatic Center, advocating for facades that engage the public street. Recent efforts include documenting the 1970 Medford High School structure and advising MassDOT on the routing of the South Medford Connector Path to favor river access.
