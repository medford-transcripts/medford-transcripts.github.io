The Historical Commission and Historic District Commission evaluate the historical and architectural significance of Medford's properties, often triggering demolition delays to explore preservation alternatives. The body oversees significant archaeological and restoration work at local cemeteries, including the Cross Street and Oak Grove sites, and manages the preservation of public spaces like Thomas Brooks Park. Throughout the recorded period, the commission has actively participated in citywide zoning reform discussions to protect historic character while allowing for development. As of October 2026, the body is focused on finalizing neighborhood surveys, reviewing certificates of appropriateness for historic districts, and adjudicating a backlog of demolition applications.

Lines of work

Demolition Review and Mitigation

ongoing

The commission reviews demolition applications for structures over a certain age, often finding them significant and imposing 18-month delays to negotiate preservation or design changes. Early cases included finding 27 Allmont Street and 38 Pearl Street preferably preserved, though delays for properties like 38 Pearl Street and 56 Wareham Street were eventually lifted following mitigated design approvals. While some structures like the fire-damaged 3-5 Pleasant Street and several properties on Mystic Avenue were cleared for demolition, others like 16 Manning Street faced strong public support for preservation. Recently, the commission allowed the demolition of the Hegner Center and 71 Mystic Avenue after determining they were not preferably preserved.

Cemetery Restoration and Archaeology

ongoing

The body manages extensive restoration projects at Medford's historic burial grounds, including headstone assessments and wall repairs. Archaeological investigations at the former Cross Street Cemetery site uncovered artifacts and human remains, leading to multi-year state coordination and site investigations. The commission has allocated funds for veteran monument restoration and explored the use of ground-penetrating radar at various sites. Ongoing work includes repairing roughly 100 relocated gravestones at Oak Grove Cemetery and seeking Community Preservation Act funding for gates and wall cleaning.

Zoning Reform and Preservation Policy

ongoing

Commissioners frequently review city and state legislation to protect historic resources from redevelopment pressures. The body discussed modeling a local preservation ordinance after Reading's zoning relief to encourage the maintenance of historic structures while increasing density. Specific focus was given to the Salem Street Corridor and Boston Avenue, where members recommended reducing building heights to protect residential character. The commission also formally opposed state Senate Bill S.993, arguing it would curtail local authority to protect historic carriage houses and district integrity.

Thomas Brooks Park and Brooks Estate

ongoing

The commission oversees the master plan and phased restoration of Thomas Brooks Park and the adjacent Brooks Estate. Phase one included the restoration of Pomp's Wall and fieldstone repairs, while phase two focused on archaeological capping, signage, and ADA-compliant sidewalk connections. The body has reviewed concept designs for a 'history walk' and coordinated maintenance efforts for the estate's stone walls and parking facilities. Archaeological surveys at the estate continue to identify sensitive areas for future preservation.

Historic District Oversight and Signage

ongoing

The body manages the regulatory requirements for established local historic districts, including the South Street and Hillside Avenue districts. Members review certificates of appropriateness for exterior alterations, such as window replacements and the construction of new accessory dwelling units. Efforts to increase public awareness included designing a homeowner informational mailer and collaborating with vocational students to create permanent district signage. The commission also explores the creation of new single-property districts, such as 44 Bradley Road, to protect specific architectural assets.

Neighborhood Historical Surveys

ongoing

To maintain accurate records, the commission funds and manages professional surveys of Medford's neighborhoods. Completed projects include the Fulton Heights survey covering 165 buildings, with subsequent work targeting Sagamore Vale, Hastings Heights, and the Mystic Valley Parkway. The commission also works on digitizing these records into a searchable local history wiki to improve public accessibility. Recent efforts have included documenting the modernist Fire Department safety complex and Medford High School for state inventory forms.
