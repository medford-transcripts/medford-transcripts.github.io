The Medford Historical Commission and Historic District Commission oversee the preservation of the city's architectural heritage, archaeological sites, and historic districts. The bodies administer the city’s demolition delay ordinance, manage specific local historic districts, and coordinate historical resource surveys to maintain Medford’s status as a Certified Local Government. Meeting from early 2023 through late 2026, the commissions have established the South Street Shipbuilding Historic District and conducted extensive archaeological assessments at Thomas Brooks Park and city cemeteries. Most recently, the commissions have focused on integrating preservation incentives into new city zoning policies and reviewing redevelopment projects for significant residential and municipal sites.

Demolition Delay and Significance Review

ongoing

The commission reviews demolition applications for structures over 75 years old to determine if they are historically significant and should be declared 'preferably preserved.' Properties found significant, such as 28 Grove Street and 110 Brooks Street, were subject to 18-month demolition delays to explore preservation or mitigation options before being released following design revisions (M04#1, M08#5, M34#1, M36#1). Other structures, including the fire-damaged buildings at 3-5 Pleasant Street and 82 Greenleaf Avenue, were cleared for demolition after the commission determined they lacked sufficient architectural integrity (M27#2, M28#6). As of late 2026, the commission continues to adjudicate significance for residential properties and municipal buildings like the Hegner Center (M53#1, M57#2).

South Street Shipbuilding Historic District

settled

The commissions initiated the creation of the South Street Shipbuilding Historic District to preserve Medford's maritime heritage, establishing boundaries and conducting extensive homeowner outreach through brochures and community events like PorchFest (M03#2, M03#3, M09#3). The body unanimously approved specific design guidelines for the district to balance historic preservation with modern materials and energy efficiency upgrades (M05#6). The district was officially supported by the commission in early 2024 and finalized after the map was filed with the Registrar of Deeds (M25#5, M38#2). Ongoing work for the district includes identifying locations for permanent historical signage and collaborating with vocational students on their design (M43#2, M46#1).

Cemetery Restoration and Archaeology

ongoing

The restoration of historic burial grounds, including the Cross Street, Oak Grove, and Salem Street cemeteries, is a major recurring focus. The commission has overseen exploratory trenching at the former Cross Street Cemetery parcel that uncovered human remains, leading to multi-year coordination with the state (M39#6). Funding was allocated for headstone assessments, the restoration of veteran monuments, and the repair of stone walls using Community Preservation Act grants (M21#5, M31#7, M40#7). The commission also required archaeological monitoring for municipal projects, such as a flagpole replacement at the Salem Street Burying Ground, to protect unknown burial sites (M33#4, M54#12).

Thomas Brooks Park and Brooks Estate

ongoing

The commission manages the archaeological preservation and master planning for Thomas Brooks Park, which contains the site of the historic Brooks family house. Phases of the project included restoring Pomp's Wall, repairing fieldstone walls, and developing a history walk with accessible paths and interpretive signage (M02#7, M23#4, M26#1). Phase two work focused on archaeological research, tree management, and ADA-compliant sidewalk connections (M35#3, M47#3). Recent activity includes conducting ground-penetrating radar surveys and appointing a liaison to the Medford Brooks Estate Land Trust to coordinate landscape and preservation needs (M52#1, M53#5).

Zoning Reform and Legislative Advocacy

ongoing

The commission frequently consults on city zoning changes to ensure the protection of historic resources in high-density corridors like Salem Street and Boston Avenue (M40#9, M49#2). Members have reviewed and supported historic preservation incentives in the Medford Square zoning ordinance, which offers dimensional waivers in exchange for design review (M47#1, M52#3). The commission also formally opposed state legislation, specifically Senate Bill S993, citing concerns that it would reduce local authority to protect historic structures from demolition (M43#4, M45#2). In 2024, the body began drafting a local preservation ordinance modeled on other municipalities to provide further zoning relief for preserving historic structures (M37#5).

Historical Resource Surveys

ongoing

To maintain federal certification, the commission regularly updates its inventory of historic resources through Form B surveys. Assessments have been completed for neighborhoods including the Brickyards, Fulton Heights, and the Boynton subdivision (M13#9, M14#6, M30#8). The commission utilizes grant funding to target specific areas, such as Hastings Heights and Sagamore Vale, to document vulnerable properties (M34#8, M40#10). In late 2026, the commission selected the Lawrence Estates area as the next priority for a state survey and planning grant (M57#6).

Design Standards and Public Education

ongoing

The commission established comprehensive design guidelines to balance historical integrity with modern requirements for solar panels, synthetic roofing, and window replacements (M01#3, M05#4). These standards prioritize visual matching and architectural character over requiring identical historical materials (M05#2, M05#3). To assist property owners, the commission introduced educational presentations on specialized topics such as PVC millwork, masonry, and insurance challenges for historic homes (M48#1, M50#4, M56#1). The body also provides advisory reviews for large-scale developments, such as the Tufts University swimming pool project, to ensure compatibility with surrounding historic contexts (M47#5).
