The Medford Historical Commission and the Medford Historic District Commission work to protect the city's architectural and cultural assets through the administration of demolition delays and the oversight of local historic districts. The bodies review demolition applications to determine historical significance, manage long-term archaeological projects at cemeteries, and advise the city on zoning reforms that impact historic corridors. Between late 2024 and late 2026, the commission shifted from drafting new preservation ordinances to finalizing specific zoning language for Boston Avenue. As of the most recent meetings, the commission is addressing a backlog of property significance hearings and developing standardized procedures for creating new local historic districts.

Demolition Delays and Significance Reviews

ongoing

The commission frequently adjudicates whether aging structures are 'preferably preserved,' which can trigger an 18-month demolition delay to explore alternatives. While some delays are lifted early due to revised design plans, such as 110 Brooks Street and 56 Wareham Street, others are invoked to protect neighborhood character, as seen at 4 Grove Street and 16 Manning Street. The commission also reviews major sites like the 1750 Benjamin Hall house and the Hegner Center, the latter of which was eventually allowed to proceed to demolition in 2026 after a public hearing. Recent activity includes a push to handle a backlog of significance hearings for multiple properties on Mystic Avenue.

Cemetery Restoration and Archaeology

ongoing

The commission manages several multi-year preservation projects focused on the city's historic burial grounds, including the Cross Street and Oak Grove Cemeteries. Early work involved assessing repair costs for relocated headstones and initiating state coordination after human remains were discovered during trenching at the Cross Street site. Funding from the Community Preservation Act has been utilized for veteran monument restoration and the cleaning of historic walls and gates. Current efforts include ground-penetrating radar testing and ongoing headstone cleaning as part of a pilot restoration program.

Zoning Reform and Preservation Ordinance

settled

To provide alternatives to demolition, the commission spent much of 2025 researching and drafting a historic preservation zoning ordinance modeled after other municipalities. The goal was to provide zoning relief for property owners who preserve historic structures while increasing density, particularly in corridors like Salem Street and Boston Avenue. Members established a subcommittee to fast-track applications for minor outbuildings like modern garages to streamline the permitting process. By late 2026, the specific zoning draft for Boston Avenue was finalized and submitted to the City Council.

Historic District Management and Expansion

ongoing

The commissions oversee several local historic districts, including the South Street and Hillside Avenue districts, requiring certificates of appropriateness for exterior alterations. Beyond established districts, the commission initiated the process to designate 44 Bradley Road as a single-property historic district. Efforts to improve district visibility include a collaboration with vocational students to design and fabricate permanent signage. Recent meetings have included educational sessions on using modern materials like PVC millwork for historic replications and planning for future district designations.

Legislative Advocacy and State Coordination

settled

The commission took a formal stance against state-level housing legislation, specifically Senate Bill S993, during the 2025 term. Members argued the bill would strip local commissions of their authority to prevent the demolition of historic accessory structures, such as carriage houses. After discussing the potential administrative burdens and impacts on historic integrity, the commission approved a formal letter to state legislators expressing their opposition. This advocacy was conducted in joint sessions to ensure a unified voice between the Historical and Historic District commissions.

Section 106 and Large-Scale Development Reviews

ongoing

The commission provides advisory comments on federal and state projects through Section 106 reviews, covering everything from cellular tower modifications to bridge restorations. Major reviews have included the South Medford Connector community path project and proposed residential redevelopments at 10-20 Revere Beach Parkway. The commission also requested earlier coordination from the Planning Department regarding large-scale institutional developments, such as proposed Tufts University dormitories. In 2026, the commission focused on historical records and photography for the Medford High School Section 106 review.

Brooks Estate Stewardship

ongoing

Working alongside the Medford Brooks Estate Land Trust, the commission monitors the maintenance and preservation of the historic manor and surrounding landscape. Early discussions focused on parking challenges and the recruitment of landscape specialists to manage the grounds. The commission formally appointed a liaison to the Land Trust to ensure consistent communication between the bodies. Recent work includes archaeological surveys identifying sensitive areas on the estate and seeking feedback from the Massachusetts Historical Commission on neighborhood survey projects.
