The CC Historical Commission archive encompasses the proceedings of Medford's historical and historic district commissions, which oversee the protection, documentation, and regulation of the city's historic assets. Meeting records in the archive span from July 2023 to October 2026. Over this time, the bodies have evaluated residential and commercial properties under the demolition delay ordinance, pursued the establishment of local historic districts, and guided Community Preservation Act projects. Their work has also directed extensive archaeological investigations and gravestone conservation at historic burying grounds and Brooks family sites. At recent meetings, the commissions addressed a backlog of demolition applications, finalized recommendations on municipal zoning initiatives, and conducted commissioner recruitment and educational programming.

Demolition delay and historic significance reviews

ongoing

The commission regularly reviews demolition applications to determine historic significance and decide whether properties should be designated as preferably preserved, invoking an 18-month demolition delay [M07#2, M09#1]. Properties subject to delay proceedings have included 56 Wareham Street, 27 Allmont Street, 38 Pearl Street, and 110 Brooks Street [M09#1, M14#1, M15#4, M20#1]. In several cases, applicants worked with subcommittees on architectural modifications and design mitigation, leading the commission to approve lifting delays for structures such as 38 Pearl Street, 110 Brooks Street, 56 Wareham Street, and 47 Holton Street [M18#1, M22#1, M23#1, M28#9]. For other properties, including 15 Maple Park Avenue and 71 Mystic Avenue, the commission determined structures were not preferably preserved after public hearings, allowing demolition to proceed [M41#1, M41#2].

South Street Local Historic District

ongoing

The Historic District Commission approved a preliminary report prepared by Chris Skelly for submission to the Massachusetts Historical Commission and organized neighborhood outreach and public hearings [M01#1, M01#2, M02#1]. The Historical Commission voted to send a letter of support for the proposed district as it advanced to the Planning Board and City Council [M11#5]. After establishing the district, commissioners noted the requirement to file the district map with the Registrar of Deeds to finalize its official status [M24#2]. The commission subsequently conducted site visits to determine locations for permanent historic markers and partnered with local vocational high school students to design and fabricate signage for the district [M29#2, M31#3, M32#1].

Cross Street Cemetery restoration and archaeology

ongoing

The commission pursued Community Preservation Act funding, historical research, and headstone restoration for Cross Street Cemetery [M03#5, M04#1, M08#5]. Exploratory trenching conducted at the former cemetery parcel uncovered artifacts and human remains, prompting multi-year state coordination, state archaeological permitting, and contracting with PAL [M20#6, M25#6]. The commission allocated funds for headstone assessments and veteran marker restoration, estimating substantial repair costs for relocated gravestones at Oak Grove Cemetery [M12#3, M25#5, M26#7]. Work later advanced to gravestone cleaning, front gate recreation plans, fence repairs, and preparations for ground-penetrating radar testing [M28#8, M33#3, M35#6, M39#5, M40#12].

Thomas Brooks Park archaeological preservation

ongoing

The commission initiated preservation plans for the archaeological site at Thomas Brooks Park, collaborating with consultant Peter Hedlund on design concepts including an archaeological cap, foundation tracing, and an accessible history walk [M08#3, M09#4, M10#3, M12#1]. Funding was secured through the Community Preservation Committee for Phase Two construction, including tree trimming, ADA sidewalk connections, and interpretive signage [M14#8, M21#3]. Consultant SWCA was selected for Phase Two archaeological work while public meetings gathered community input on park design and natural features [M21#4, M25#8]. Preservation efforts continued into 2026 with completed tree work and plans for ground-penetrating radar [M33#3, M35#6].

Preservation ordinances and citywide zoning coordination

ongoing

The commission examined preservation zoning models from other municipalities, such as Reading, to explore zoning relief incentives for property owners who preserve historic structures [M22#3, M23#5]. Commissioners monitored proposed municipal up-zoning, including the Salem Street corridor, and formed subcommittees to comment on proposed city zoning changes [M26#9, M27#5]. The bodies drafted opposition to state legislation, Senate Bill S993, citing concerns regarding restrictions on local oversight over historic demolitions [M29#4, M31#2]. Commission members later reviewed adopted Medford Square zoning incentives and finalized recommendations for the Boston Avenue zoning corridor [M33#1, M35#2, M40#10].

Historical survey projects and inventory documentation

ongoing

The commission funded and conducted MHC Form B historical surveys and neighborhood area surveys across Medford, including the Brickyards, Fulton Heights, Bradley Hall Estates, Sagamore Vale, and Hastings Heights [M03#5, M10#6, M11#7, M16#8, M20#8, M22#6, M25#8]. It also voted to recommend Shiloh Baptist Church and Oak Grove Cemetery structures for National Register eligibility reviews, with Shiloh Baptist Church determined eligible [M09#3, M15#7]. Efforts were undertaken to modernize records by designing a searchable digital database and local history wiki using inventory forms [M27#8, M28#5, M30#7]. Additional documentation surveys were authorized for properties undergoing development, such as Medford High School and the Brooks Estate neighborhood [M35#6, M40#11, M40#13].

Brooks Estate preservation and land trust coordination

ongoing

The commission maintained recurring coordination with the Brooks Estate and the Medford Brooks Estate Land Trust regarding capital improvements, estate operations, and archaeological preservation [M07#5, M15#9, M22#7]. Members addressed site infrastructure challenges, including DPW stone wall maintenance, estate parking facilities, and coordination with an adjacent cemetery parcel design [M16#7, M22#7, M25#10, M33#4]. In 2026, the commission appointed former commissioner Ben Johnson as its official liaison to the Medford Brooks Estate Land Trust [M38#1]. Commissioners also advanced bid preparations for permeable parking pavement and reviewed an archaeological survey identifying sensitive areas on the estate [M35#8, M38#5, M39#5].
