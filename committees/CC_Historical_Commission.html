The Medford Historical Commission and the Medford Historic District Commission manage the city's architectural heritage by overseeing the demolition delay process and regulating local historic districts. Between 2022 and 2026, these bodies evaluated the historical significance of numerous properties and managed large-scale preservation projects for municipal parks and cemeteries. Their work included establishing the South Street Shipbuilding Historic District, executing a master plan for Thomas Brooks Park, and documenting hundreds of properties through neighborhood surveys. At the most recent meetings, the commission focused on integrating preservation incentives into city zoning reforms and managing long-term archaeological investigations at the former Cross Street Cemetery.

South Street Shipbuilding Historic District

settled

In late 2022, the commission initiated a project to protect the South Street neighborhood through a local historic district designation. The body hired a preservation consultant to draft a preliminary study report and developed comprehensive design guidelines for the area. Outreach efforts included community events like PorchFest and direct mailers to property owners regarding Medford's shipbuilding heritage. The designation process concluded in late 2024 when the final district map was filed with the Registrar of Deeds. Subsequent work shifted to planning permanent historic signage in collaboration with vocational students.

Demolition Delay and Ordinance Reform

ongoing

The commission regularly reviews demolition applications to determine if structures are historically significant and should be preferably preserved. When a property is found significant, the commission can impose an 18-month demolition delay to explore alternatives to destruction. Members frequently met with city building commissioners to coordinate permit routing and enforce bylaw compliance. Over time, the commission established subcommittees to streamline the review process, particularly for minor outbuildings and pre-1900 structures. Recent efforts have focused on fast-tracking applications for modern garages and sheds while maintaining strict oversight for character-defining residential features.

Cemetery Restoration and Archaeology

ongoing

A multi-year effort is underway to restore historic burial grounds and conduct archaeological assessments at various sites. The commission secured funding for headstone repairs at Cross Street and Oak Grove cemeteries and initiated ground-penetrating radar testing to identify unknown burial locations. A significant discovery occurred in early 2025 when exploratory trenching at the former Cross Street Cemetery parcel uncovered human remains, leading to long-term state coordination. The body also funded the restoration of veteran headstones through specialized grant programs. Current work involves cleaning stone walls and recreating historical front gates at cemetery sites.

Thomas Brooks Park Master Plan

ongoing

The commission oversees the preservation of Thomas Brooks Park, focusing on its colonial and indigenous history. Early work centered on restoring fieldstone walls and renaming the 'Slave Wall' to 'Pomp's Wall' to honor its enslaved builder. A multi-phase master plan was developed that includes an accessible history walk, interpretive signage, and an archaeological cap to protect the site of the former Brooks estate house. Phase two of the project involved tree maintenance, ADA sidewalk connections, and further archaeological research. Recent updates presented conceptual designs for a history walk featuring stone block outlines of historic structures.

Neighborhood Historical Surveys

ongoing

The commission continuously seeks state and local funding to conduct neighborhood-wide surveys of historic resources. These projects result in the creation of Massachusetts Historical Commission Form B inventory forms for hundreds of individual properties. Major surveys were completed for the Brickyards, Fulton Heights, and Boynton subdivision areas to document their architectural and social histories. The body has also worked toward creating a searchable digital wiki database to make these inventory forms accessible to the public. Most recently, the commission selected Lawrence Estates as the next priority area for a state survey and planning grant.

Zoning and Legislative Advocacy

ongoing

The commission actively engages with city and state policy changes that impact historic preservation. Locally, members worked to incorporate preservation incentives and dimensional waivers into the Medford Square and Boston Avenue zoning reform packages. The body also expressed formal opposition to State Senate Bill S993, arguing that proposed housing legislation would undermine local authority to protect historic structures like carriage houses. Members have advocated for character-defining protections within the Salem Street corridor to mitigate the impacts of proposed up-zoning. Current work includes drafting a new local ordinance to provide zoning relief for property owners who choose to preserve historic structures.
