The Medford Bicycle Advisory Commission is a civic body that advises the city on bicycle infrastructure, safety, and public outreach. Since 2022, the commission has worked to expand Medford's bicycle network through new lanes, bike-share stations, and dedicated multi-use paths like the Clippership Connector. The body also coordinates the procurement and distribution of safety equipment, such as lights and helmets, and advocates for safety improvements on state-controlled roadways. As of late 2026, the commission is focused on strategic goals involving contraflow lanes, membership recruitment, and establishing formal safety reporting protocols with the police department.

State Roadway Advocacy

ongoing

The body frequently advocates for safety improvements on roadways controlled by state agencies like MassDOT and the DCR. Early efforts included responding to fatal crashes on Middlesex Avenue and reviewing the Wellington Circle redesign study to prioritize non-motorized users. The commission has voiced opposition to substandard infrastructure, such as the 2025 repainting of the Route 60 rotary, and requested lower speed limits on state-controlled routes. Efforts are ongoing to coordinate with neighboring towns like Arlington on unified safety marking proposals for border rotaries.

Police Collaboration and Safety Reporting

ongoing

Safety advocacy expanded from infrastructure to driver behavior following reports of cyclist harassment and road rage incidents. The commission formed a working group to coordinate with the Mayor and the police department on improving incident reporting and law enforcement training. Members have pushed for specific categories in the SeeClickFix system to track bicycle-related harassment and dooring collisions. Advocacy efforts also include requesting recurring bicycle patrols and better data tracking from the police traffic division.

Paths and Trail Development

ongoing

The commission tracks the development of major multi-use paths, most notably the Clippership Connector and the Mystic River Pedestrian Bridge. Members reviewed designs for the Connector, noting construction delays that pushed its opening to 2025, and advocated for wayfinding signage through Medford Square. The body also supported a bicycle pump track proposal for Carr Park and monitored shared-use path connections under Route 28. Regional connectivity remains a priority, including links to the Northern Strand and the Minuteman path extension.
