Public bicycle rack expansion

ongoing

The commission works with municipal departments to site and install bicycle racks across Medford using stored inventory and grant awards (M03#4, M04#6). After the city secured a grant for 40 bike racks, the commission assisted with field siting and developed installation guidelines for private businesses (M06#4, M07#4). Members also sought a dedicated municipal budget line item for racks and asked the city to add bike rack requests to SeeClickFix (M05#5, M13#3, M13#4). Work continued through 2025 and 2026 to track delayed grant installations, request cost estimates from city staff, and address localized rack maintenance complaints (M10#6, M11#6, M14#4, M16#11).

Police engagement and road safety advocacy

ongoing

In September 2024, the commission voted to establish a working group to coordinate with the mayor and police department on responses to aggressive driving and driver harassment following a road rage incident (M09#4, M10#2). The working group sought dedicated bicycle patrols, officer safety training, and the addition of a harassment category to the city's SeeClickFix platform (M12#6, M13#4). Following a cyclist fatality in mid-2026, the commission formed another working group to engage police on safety enforcement, incident tracking, and dooring collision reporting (M16#9, M17#2). In September 2026, the body outlined formal advocacy priorities to bring to an upcoming meeting with the interim police chief (M18#11).

Clippership Connector and shared-use paths

ongoing

The commission tracked construction progress and design specifications for the off-road Clippership Connector, which experienced delays before its official opening in mid-2025 (M04#6, M07#6, M09#3, M12#3). Following the opening, the commission pushed back against dismount signs and deployed temporary wayfinding signs through Medford Square (M12#9, M13#8). The body also monitored associated contraflow connections and regional trail links, including the Wellington riverfront path and Mystic River Greenway (M14#7, M16#8, M18#10).

State roadway safety and rotary redesigns

ongoing

The commission frequently advocates for safety upgrades on state corridors controlled by the Department of Conservation and Recreation and MassDOT (M03#3, M03#6, M08#4). In late 2024, members voted to endorse lowering speed limits to 25 miles per hour on state-owned roads in Medford and supported requests for state safety improvements (M10#4, M10#6, M11#5). Following an unannounced repainting of the Route 60 rotary in 2025 that the commission deemed substandard, the board sent a protest letter to DCR and partnered with Arlington's advisory committee on joint safety marking proposals (M13#9, M14#2). The commission continues to monitor state projects, including MassDOT path underpasses and Mystic River bridge contracts, while seeking improved coordination during state design phases (M17#4, M18#2).

ongoing

In 2024, the commission requested the city's repaving schedule from the Engineering Department to identify opportunities for adding bicycle infrastructure during road maintenance (M09#5).
